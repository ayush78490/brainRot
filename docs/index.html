    Under the hood

    How it counts.

    Android lets accessibility apps see basic events, such as "this list scrolled to item 17". Brainrot uses exactly that and nothing more.

    1. Spots Reels

      It knows you're in Reels when the Reels tab is tapped or the Reels pager moves. Leave Reels, switch tabs or close Instagram, and it stops.

    2. Counts new reels

      When the pager lands on a reel number it hasn't seen yet, that's +1. Instagram reports the position itself, so the count is exact.

    3. Saves every one

      Each reel is saved with its time, so today's total, your sittings and the hourly chart are always worked out from real events.

    1. Download the APK on your phone

      Tap Download APK. When Chrome says "File might be harmful", tap Download anyway. It says that about every app that doesn't come from the Play Store.

    2. Open it and allow the install

      Open brainrot.apk from your notifications or the Downloads folder. The first time, Android asks you to allow installs from your browser:

      Settings→Allow from this source→back→Install

    3. Play Protect: open "More details"

      After you tap Install, Google Play Protect shows "App scan recommended" with two big buttons. Don't use either. Tap More details, then Install without scanning.

      More details ⌄→Install without scanning

      Some phones show "Unsafe app blocked" instead. Same idea: More details, then Install anyway.

      Blocked with no option at all? Turn off scanning for a minute: Play Store → your profile picture → Play Protect → ⚙ → turn off Scan apps with Play Protect, install Brainrot, then turn it back on. Play Protect flags apps from outside the Play Store that use the Accessibility API. Brainrot never reads your passwords or messages, and sends nothing until you choose to add friends.

    4. Allow restricted settings Android 13+

      Newer Android versions grey out Accessibility for apps installed from a file. If Brainrot is greyed out in the next step, unlock it first:

      Settings→Apps→Brainrot→⋮ top-right→Allow restricted settings

      Can't see the ⋮ menu? Try turning Brainrot on in Accessibility once. Once you've been blocked, the option appears.

    5. Turn on the Brainrot service

      Open Brainrot and tap the red Off pill at the top. It takes you straight to Accessibility.

      Installed appsorDownloaded apps→Brainrot→On→Allow

      The warning says the app can "view and control screen". That's the standard wording for every accessibility app. Brainrot only reads which list scrolled and its position, never text.

    6. Let it run in the background

      Otherwise your phone's battery saver will quietly stop it, and the app will say Not running.

      Settings→Apps→Brainrot→Battery→Unrestricted
      Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus and others
      • Samsung: App info → Battery → Unrestricted. Also check it isn't under Settings → Battery → Background usage limits → Sleeping apps.
      • Xiaomi / Redmi / POCO: App info → Battery saver → No restrictions, and turn on Autostart.
      • OnePlus / OPPO / realme: App info → Battery usage → allow background activity.
      • Anything else: look for "battery optimisation" and set Brainrot to Don't optimise. dontkillmyapp.com has steps for every brand.

    7. Open Reels and scroll

      Go to Instagram, tap Reels and swipe. The brain pops up in the top-right corner and starts counting. Back in Brainrot, the pill should be green and say Tracking.

      Optional: tap Add to home screen under the count for the widget.

    Privacy

    It watches the scroll bar, not you.

    What it reads

    • Which app is on screen, so it knows when you leave Instagram
    • The internal name of the list that scrolled, such as the Reels pager
    • The position number Android attaches to that scroll
    • Which Instagram tab you tapped

    What it never touches

    • Captions, comments, messages, or anyone else's username
    • Screenshots, recordings, or anything you type
    • The internet, unless you connect for friends. Then only your username, name and daily reel totals.
    • Your Instagram account. You never log in to anything.
    FAQ

    Questions, answered.

    Why does it need Accessibility?

    Accessibility is the only way an Android app can tell that another app scrolled. Brainrot subscribes to three event types (scrolled, window changed, tab selected), reads the list's name and position, and ignores everything else.

    Why isn't it on the Play Store?

    Google Play only allows the Accessibility API for a narrow set of uses, and a Reels counter isn't one of them. So Brainrot is shared as an APK you install yourself.

    The count stopped going up. What happened?

    Most likely, Android stopped the service to save battery. Open Brainrot: if the pill says Not running, turn the service off and on again in Accessibility, and set battery to Unrestricted (step 6).

    If the pill says Tracking and it still doesn't count, an Instagram update probably renamed something Brainrot looks for. Tap Export debug capture at the bottom of the app and open an issue on GitHub with the file. It contains no captions, names or messages.

    Does it work on every phone?

    It's been verified on a OnePlus phone running Android 15 with Instagram 447. Other Android 10+ phones should work the same way, but they haven't all been tested. If yours doesn't count, the debug export helps fix it.

    Does it count the reel I land on when I open Reels?

    No, it counts swipes. Open Reels and swipe 17 times, and you get 17.

    Does it slow my phone down or drain the battery?

    It shouldn't. It never reads your screen or scans the view tree, and events from other apps are dropped straight away. The busiest thing it does is save one small database row per reel.

    How do I uninstall it?

    Like any app: long-press the icon and tap Uninstall. The service and all its data go with it.
